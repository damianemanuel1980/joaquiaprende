Mini teoría: Past Simple y Past Continuous

Cuando contamos algo que pasó en el pasado, en inglés podemos usar dos formas principales: Past Simple y Past Continuous.

Past Simple

Usamos Past Simple para hablar de una acción que empezó y terminó en el pasado. Es una acción completa.

The wolf knocked on the door.
El lobo golpeó la puerta.

Verbos regulares

En Past Simple, muchos verbos son regulares. Eso significa que forman el pasado agregando -ed.

walk → walked

Algunos cambian un poquito la escritura: dance → danced, cry → cried, stop → stopped.

Verbos irregulares

Otros verbos son irregulares. No agregan -ed: cambian de forma.

run → ran

Estos verbos se aprenden de memoria, poco a poco.

Past Continuous

Usamos Past Continuous para hablar de una acción que estaba ocurriendo en un momento del pasado.

Se forma así: was / were + verbo-ing.

The wolf was knocking on the door.
El lobo estaba golpeando la puerta.

Was o were

En Past Continuous usamos was o were según el sujeto.

I / he / she / it → was

The wolf was running.

you / we / they → were

They were hiding.

Cuando usamos los dos juntos

Muchas veces usamos Past Continuous y Past Simple en la misma oración.

El Past Continuous muestra la acción larga, la que estaba pasando. El Past Simple muestra la acción corta, la que ocurrió de repente.

The wolf was knocking when the pig opened the door.

was knocking = estaba pasando

opened = pasó y terminó

Idea clave: Past Simple = algo pasó y terminó. Past Continuous = algo estaba pasando.

Cómo resolver

  • 1

    Mirá el verbo entre paréntesis.

    Ese es el verbo que tenés que cambiar.

  • 2

    Pensá la acción.

    Si la acción pasó y terminó → Past Simple. Si estaba pasando → Past Continuous. Si hay dos acciones, la más larga suele ir en Past Continuous y la más corta en Past Simple.

  • 3

    Past Simple:

    Verbo regular → agregá -ed. Verbo irregular → cambia de forma.

  • 4

    Past Continuous:

    was / were + verbo-ing.

  • 5

    Pistas:

    while suele acompañar la acción larga. when suele marcar la acción corta. Primero entendé la oración: no completes automático.

Exercise

Tip: use the verb in brackets. Write Past Simple or Past Continuous.

Example: While the Wolf was baking the cake, he ran out of sugar.

A
The Wolf (knock) on the door when he felt a sneeze coming.

Look at the two actions. Which one was already happening when the sneeze came?

B
While the Second Pig (shave) his chin, the Wolf arrived at the wooden house.

The word while usually points to the longer action that was in progress.

C
When the straw house (fall), the Wolf was looking for the pig.

This action happened suddenly. Remember: fall is an irregular verb.

D
While he (walk) to the brick house, the Wolf felt very sick.

Ask yourself: which action was happening for more time?

E
While the third pig (cook) dinner, the Wolf knocked on the door.

Use the clue while: it usually goes with the action that was already happening.

F
While the Wolf was blowing down the straw house, the First Pig (run) to his brother's house.

The wolf's action was in progress. The pig's action happened and finished. Run is irregular.

G
While the Third Pig (build) the brick house, his brothers were playing.

The word while gives you a clue: building was the longer action.

H
The little pigs were hiding under the table when the Wolf (sneeze).

One action was already happening. The other action happened suddenly. Sneeze is regular.
